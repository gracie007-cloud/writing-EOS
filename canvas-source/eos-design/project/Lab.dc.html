How a step gets its experts

Primary
You set it in the Narrative Components database, on the evidence of the rounds. A cheaper model that is comparable for the job goes first.
Comparable
The second expert in the line. At the Full tier it cross-checks the Primary.
Fallback
The third expert: the one named for the step, or the one the roster gives its Primary. A model on a second provider is a second route.
Mature tier
Uncensored models on Featherless AI. Checkers keep a Mature Fallback for work their experts refuse.
Out of a round
More than one refusal or cut-off answer in three runs.

Judging

You score prose by default. A model judge never runs on the model that wrote the output it judges, whichever provider serves it, and there are no anthropic or qwen judges in the opening chapter test.

Calibrating the fit scores

Conflict Fit (out of 27) and Structure Fit (out of 36) start from your Notion weights. Run 15–20 known stories through them; where the pick keeps missing the author’s real choice, adjust the weight responsible and log it here.

No calibration runs yet Each tested story will show the model’s pick, the author’s choice and any weight you change.

Where the assignments live

The Primary, Comparable and Fallback of each step are set in the Narrative Components database, and the app follows it. Model names written on older agent pages are ideas to test.

  • Rounds are evidence: a round ranks the candidates for a step, and you move a model up or down the line on that evidence.
  • The roster holds the facts: provider, model ID, Uncensored, Genre and Modality for every model. A model listed under two providers has two routes.
  • What cannot run yet: the readiness report lists every step with nothing it could call, and the reason.
