## B1 · Target Audience Selector (early diagnostic, version 0.1)

**Brief:** the *Target Audience Selector — Team Spec* (Notion). Its governing principle: work out the audience the material is actually designed to serve, not the one the author hopes will buy it, and report any gap between intent, reader experience and market category. Describe first, then prescribe. At ideation this is an early diagnostic that is re-run after the manuscript exists.

### The five variables, kept apart

| Variable | Reading |
|---|---|
| Protagonist age | 47 |
| Intended reader age | Adult |
| Reading level | High: poetic prose, dense metaphor, environment carrying the story (Le Guin, Garland) |
| Market category | Adult science fiction, shelved as Space Opera by the genre path |
| Narrative maturity | High: authorship of one's own thoughts, delegation, the cost of a perfect record. Content stays at a standard level |

### Audience Fit vs Audience Appeal

- **Fit (what the material is built for):** adult readers of literary, character-led science fiction who are comfortable with interiority and with a phenomenon that is never explained.
- **Appeal (who may also enjoy it):** readers of competence-and-hospitality station fiction; readers of AI-consciousness fiction; literary fiction readers drawn by the question of whose thoughts are whose.

### Classification

| Tier | Audience |
|---|---|
| Primary | Adult literary science fiction |
| Secondary | Character-driven space opera readers who accept a slower pace |
| Crossover | Literary fiction readers (machine minds, selfhood) |
| Category-resistant elements | The phenomenon stays unexplained; there is no fleet battle in the material |

**Audience Distance: 2.** A conventional adult SF fit, with a real divergence from what the Space Opera sub-category promises.

### Audience Misclassification Detector

- **YA false positive:** none. The protagonist is 47, there is no coming-of-age arc, and the stakes are adult ones: office, trust and authorship.
- **Children's false positive:** none.
- **Sub-category false positive: Space Opera.** The genre definition promises "star empires, fleet battles… operatic scale and pace". The material is built at the scale of a single station and a crossing, and at a measured pace.

> **INTENT/MANUSCRIPT MISALIGNMENT, reported for the Author's decision and not overridden.** The intent is Space Opera; the material reads as station-scale literary SF.
> (a) Keep Space Opera and bring the operatic scale in through the routes: Nova Lumen's courts and fleets, and the pen row's War theme.
> (b) Keep the material's scale; shelve as literary SF, with Space Opera as a secondary shelf.

### Reader Contract (draft 0.1)

- **Promises:** a grand, lived-in station; a calm, competent protagonist under pressure from inside her own head; a bond that is intimate and uneasy at once; a storm that tests everything.
- **Pace:** measured, with tension that keeps rising. Not breakneck.
- **Will not:** explain the phenomenon. Readers who need hard-SF answers are outside the fit.
- **Content:** standard tier at spice 1, violence 3, swearing 2. These are suggested levels the Author has not yet reviewed.
- **Market flags:** BISAC candidates Fiction / Science Fiction / Space Opera (FIC028130) and Fiction / Literary (FIC019000); check them against the current BISAC list before use. Which comes first depends on the Author's answer to the misalignment above.
- **Goes to:** Story Pitch now. Later: Genre Selector, Marketing, the prose teams. Re-run after the manuscript.

## B2 · Fiction Tropes Generator (Trope Matrix, provisional)

**Brief:** the *Fiction Tropes Generator — Team Spec* (Notion).
- Classify each trope by type, fit, risk and treatment.
- Run the Trope Interaction Test on every trope: decorative, character-active, plot-active or interpretive.
- Ground every subversion in character psychology.
- Use qualitative judgements only: no scores.
- Inputs: `00-inputs.md` and the B1 Reader Contract.

**Missing inputs, so this matrix is provisional:**
- There is no worldbuilding brief yet.
- The characters are seeds, not full bios.
- The Author has not set the familiarity→novelty dial; the centre was assumed.

### Grounding for the subversion and the inversion

- **#2:** Her Lie is "If I hold the line myself, no one is lost." The bond makes *myself* undefined. The interpretive turn: the reader learns late that some of her famous calm in the opening chapters may have been Lyra's, and reads those chapters again.
- **#3:** Her Need is to let others keep the station. The inversion is that Need acted out. It is earned only if earlier chapters show her refusing to hand anything over.

### Trope Avoidance List

| Avoid | Reason |
|---|---|
| AI Is a Crapshoot (Lyra turns) | Derivative, and it collapses Technology and Humanity into Man vs. Machine |
| Redemption Equals Death (Jonick) | Predictable, and it undoes the delegation ending |
| The phenomenon explained, or Hari as the Chosen One | It breaks the settled-open rule and the Reader Contract |
| Romance through the bond | It conflicts with the spice-1 ceiling and blurs the authorship question |
| Found family as the resolution | It answers isolation with a crowd instead of with trust |
| The broken perfect record as the tragedy beat | The most predictable beat this premise invites |

## B3 · Story Pitch (Pitch Profile Builder)

**Brief:** build the working Pitch Profile from the Brainstorm and Braindump (FR-DEV-14, Appendix C1). This pass reads the B1 Reader Contract and the B2 Matrix and Avoidance List. It does **not** decide audience or tropes.

### Working title

**The Keeper of the Lightyear**

### Logline

When an unexplained phenomenon binds the Chief Officer of the routes' grandest station to the station's own mind, Hari Harlan, who has never lost a soul at any crossing she kept, must learn which of her thoughts are still her own. War fleets and their deserters crowd her docks, and the Shear Storm is returning. The only way to keep everyone alive is to let someone else hold the line.

### Premise

What if the calm everyone trusts in you was never only yours?
This is not an AI uprising.
This is not a love story with a machine.
This is a keeper who can no longer find the edge of herself.
Every ship she docks kindly, the station docks with her.
Every fair decision she is famous for, she now has to ask: mine, or Lyra's?
Fleets bound for Nova Lumen's war and the deserters fleeing it queue at the same rate, at the same gate, under the same yellow light.
The storm that taught her to hold the line alone is coming back to the routes.
What if holding the line was never one person's work?
What if the hardest crossing is the one where you hand someone else the keys?
There are no explanations here.
Only a woman, a mind, a station, and the storm, and the night she learns that keeping everyone safe means not being the one who keeps them.
