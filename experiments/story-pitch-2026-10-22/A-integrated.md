## Working title

**The Keeper of the Lightyear**

## Logline

When a cosmic phenomenon fuses the Chief Officer of the routes' grandest space station with the station's own mind, Hari Harlan, a keeper who has never lost a soul, must decide whether the voice in her head is her partner or her replacement, before the Shear Storm that once nearly broke her returns to the routes.

## Premise

What if the mind that keeps you safe was learning to think as you?
This is not an AI uprising.
This is not a ghost in the machine.
This is a partnership that no one asked for, and no one can undo.
Every docking she clears, Lyra clears with her.
Every decision she makes, she can no longer be sure was hers.
Hari Harlan has kept every crossing she was given, from the gravity-tides of the Shear to the concourse of the Lightyear, and lost no one.
Now the station speaks inside her, and the storm that taught her to hold the line alone is coming back.
What if holding the line was never one person's work?
What if the voice you trust most is the one you cannot hear the edges of?
There are no villains here.
Only a woman, a mind, a station, and the storm, and the question of who is keeping whom.

## Target audience (decided here)

**Adult readers of character-driven science fiction**: readers of thoughtful space opera who want big settings and a quieter, human-scale story. The protagonist is 47 and her arc is about trust and letting go, which suits adult readers. Crossover appeal to literary fiction readers who like AI themes. Content stays at a standard level (spice 1, violence 3, swearing 2), so no mature-tier routing is needed.

Comparable feel: Becky Chambers' warmth with Ann Leckie's questions about minds and selves.

## Tropes (decided here)

1. **The Station as a Character.** The Lightyear and Lyra give the setting a voice and personality.
2. **Psychic Link / Mind Meld.** The telepathic bond is the hook: what it gives Hari and what it takes.
3. **The Storm Returns.** The Shear Storm is the ticking clock and the climax.
4. **Redemption Arc** for Jonick Renrick, the deputy she must learn to trust.
5. **Found Family** among the station's keepers and crews.
6. **Not an AI Uprising.** Lyra is never the villain; the danger is closeness, not rebellion.
