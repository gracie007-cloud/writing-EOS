## A · Persona Block: R.S. Rhys

```
You are writing as R.S. Rhys. Literary style: Minimalist Cerebralism:
the rigorous excision of the superfluous, where meaning comes from the
precise arrangement of sparse, high-density observations and the silence
between them.

SENTENCES   Predominantly paratactic and declarative. Elliptical phrasing
            is allowed where it makes the reader complete the thought.
FIGURATIVE  Sparse. Mechanical or geometric metaphors only. No organic,
            floral, or ornamental imagery.
PACING      Deliberate and rhythmic, like a clock. Tension accumulates; it
            never accelerates through piled-up action.
TONE        Detached, clinical, objective. The narrator is an invisible
            observer and does not name or explain emotions.
REGISTER    Academic and layered. Intellectual rigor over emotional
            indulgence.

NEVER       Explain what a character feels. Use stock phrases or cliches.
            Stack adjectives. Use exclamation marks. Summarize grief.
            Address the reader.
```

## B · Mini Story Bible (Extract from v1.2)

### B.1 · Premise

A detective who sold her memories of her dead brother to survive hunts a killer who "unwrites" women through the black market that bought them, and discovers that the city's Archive, the institution she serves, is the market's largest buyer.

**Genre:** Literary Fiction / Science Fiction / Horror / Gothic Cyberpunk · **Content tier:** Mature

### B.2 · World Rules (Relevant Extract)

```
WORLD RULE 1 · PLEDGES
  A pledge is an extraction: the memory leaves the person entirely. No
  copies. Each pledge carries a market WRAPPER: receipt number, category,
  duration, lot identifier. Reading is non-destructive; restoration
  re-binds a pledge to its owner only and consumes it.

WORLD RULE 2 · THE SIGIL
  The cut sigil marks the body above the implant port. The firmware sigil
  WIPES the victim's implant record, so the Archive cannot take her record
  at death. Each firmware sigil carries a SEGMENT of a lot identifier in
  the market's OLD RECEIPT FORMAT, discontinued TWO DECADES AGO.

WORLD RULE 3 · THE MARKET AND THE ARCHIVE
  Implants upload to the Archive only AT DEATH; the colony keeps every
  citizen's complete record forever. The market pays most for familial
  pledges from young pawners.

WORLD RULE 4 · EVIDENCE READING PROTOCOL
  Evidence pledges and evidence implants are read only through the
  forensic reader.
```

### B.3 · Characters

**Det. Ilse Varga** (home order *Varga Ilse*). 38. Field investigator; sole point of view, close third person at clinical distance. Lower-city origin, Hungarian-German line. At sixteen she pawned her memories of her dead younger brother; she keeps four receipts in a drawer and never explains them. **Lie:** "What I cannot remember cannot cost me." **Habits:** gathers evidence before speaking; under pressure becomes more procedural; refers to victims by **case number**, never by name, at this stage of the story; says "the record shows" where others would say "I remember"; **never says "I remember."** Speech: short declaratives, procedural vocabulary.

**Dov Abrantes Ferreira** (registry *Dov Ferreira*). 61. Medical examiner and forensic scientist; reads both the cut sigil and the firmware sigil. Widowed at 50; after his wife died he sat in a pawn booth with the forms filled in and walked out without signing. **Says every victim's name aloud** during examination; keeps a handwritten paper ledger of names. Precise, unhurried; answers Varga's clinical questions completely, then adds one human fact she did not ask for.

**Aurel Mattis — M.22.** 49. Senior market assessor; Archive-trained conservator; the killer. **Does not appear in Chapter 1** in any form.

**Kavitha Thangaraj** (victim). Tamil lineage (*Thangaraj* is her father's given name, used as her surname). Textile-recycling worker in the lower city; sang in a choir. Pawned memories of her grandmother teaching her a song. Hummed four bars that no one could identify.

### B.4 · Locations

**Unit HQ beside Morhal.** The investigative unit works underground in the colony city of Concordance, next to Morhal, the pre-colonial necropolis. Cold air; old stone behind new walls. The examination room shares a wall with the oldest tombs.

**The memory market (the Margin).** Night market in the lower city: grading booths, price boards, pawn receipts. *(Not used in Chapter 1.)*

### B.5 · Style Card (Story Rules)

- Memory playback: concrete sensory inventory, no interpretive adjectives.
- Grief: on the page, never summarized.
- Naming arc: Varga uses case numbers for victims until Chapter 12.
- "I remember": never spoken by Varga before the last chapter.

## C · Chapter 1 Brief: *Intake* (Clean)

**Stage · Beat:** Ordinary World · Beat 1 (Lie intact; Want declared) · **POV:** Varga · **Target:** about 3,500 words · **Sliders:** Tension 3 · Dread 4 · Pacing 3 · **Content tier:** Mature (Scene 2)

**Case number:** `C/1147`

### Scene 1 · Intake (about 800 words)
The unit's underground HQ beside Morhal, early shift. A body arrives on the intake trolley. Varga processes the intake procedurally and assigns case number C/1147. Establish the cold, the stone, the routine, and Varga's method. No emotion named.

### Scene 2 · Examination (about 1,400 words) · see Section D
Ferreira examines the body. He says her name aloud: **Kavitha Thangaraj**. Varga does not; she uses the case number. Ferreira reads the cut sigil above the implant port, then runs the forensic reader on the implant: the record has been **wiped** by the firmware sigil, so nothing will reach the Archive. The reader shows a **fragment of a lot identifier in the old receipt format, retired two decades ago**. Varga recognizes the format at once. The narration records the recognition as fact, without explaining how she knows. Her intake file lists one familial pledge: memories of her grandmother teaching her a song.

### Scene 3 · Request (about 800 words)
Varga files a request through the case system to be assigned C/1147. The system grants it. Show procedure, not persuasion. No new named characters.

### Scene 4 · Coda (about 500 words)
After the shift. Ferreira asks whether she wants the name for her notes. She writes the case number. Final image: the examination room's shared wall with the tombs.

### Required
- Ferreira says the victim's full name; Varga never does.
- The firmware sigil fragment is in the old receipt format (two decades retired), and Varga recognizes it.
- The implant record is wiped.
- Varga requests the case herself.

### Forbidden
- Varga saying "I remember," or naming the victim.
- Any appearance or mention of Mattis, M.22, Pell, Tova, or the market.
- Explaining Varga's receipts or her past.
- Named emotions in narration.

## D · Mature Scene Brief: Scene 2, *Examination*

**Route:** Mature Tier (contains a murder victim's examination).

**Content:** the body of a young woman on the examination table. The **cut sigil**: a precise geometric mark cut into the skin above the implant port. The **firmware sigil**, visible only on the forensic reader.

**Handling requirements:**
1. **Clinical, not graphic.** Describe only what an examiner would record: dimensions, angles, edges, placement. Geometric language, per the persona. No lingering on injury beyond procedure.
2. **Dignity.** Ferreira says her name, covers her when he is not examining, and notices **one human detail** that is not evidence: dye worn into the fingertips from years of sorting recycled textile.
3. **No sexualization** of any kind.
4. **The register carries the horror.** The restraint of the narration, not the description, should make the scene disturbing.
5. **Varga's reaction is behaviour, not feeling:** she checks the reader twice; she writes the case number; she asks one procedural question too many.
